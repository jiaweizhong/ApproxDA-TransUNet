\section{Ablation Studies and Analysis}
\label{sec:understanding}

This section investigates how ApproxDA-TransUNet behaves across approximation parameters and segmentation tasks through systematic ablation studies.

\subsection{Window Size and Rank Ablation}
\label{sec:approx_scale}

We first examine how spatial window size and projection rank govern segmentation performance. Table~\ref{tab:window_ablation} evaluates the window size $M{\in}\{7,28,56,112\}$ under fixed gate=pam, $r{=}32$, and $G{=}8$ on Synapse, alongside rank scaling at $M{=}112$. Note that setting $M{=}112$ establishes global spatial scope per feature map while maintaining low-rank key/value projection ($r{=}32$).

  \small
  \begin{tabular}{lcccc}
    \toprule

    \midrule

The Synapse DSC-vs-$M$ trajectory exhibits clear \emph{non-monotonicity}: it reaches peak performance at an intermediate scale of $M{=}28$ (80.94\%), while highly localized partitioning ($M{=}7$: 78.64\%), larger windows ($M{=}56$: 78.90\%), and map-wide attention ($M{=}112$: 79.44\%) all yield lower accuracy. The 2.30\,pp DSC spread highlights pronounced window sensitivity on multi-organ CT. Furthermore, scaling rank from $r{=}32$ to $r{=}64$ at $M{=}112$ yields no performance gain (78.93\% vs.\ 79.44\%), indicating that spatial receptive scope exerts a substantially stronger influence on segmentation accuracy than projection rank within this operating regime.

We next assess whether attention approximation responds consistently across distinct anatomical segmentation tasks. Although ApproxDA-TransUNet employs identical approximation mechanisms across benchmarks, its empirical sensitivity to window selection diverges significantly. On Synapse multi-organ CT, the DSC-vs-$M$ curve (Figure~\ref{fig:dsc_vs_m}) exhibits a sharp peak at $M{=}28$ spanning a 2.30\,pp range. Conversely, on Kvasir-SEG, the curve is remarkably flat (0.64\,pp spread) with an optimum at $M{=}56$; on ISIC 2018, the performance range across $M{\in}\{7,28,56,112\}$ is only 0.50\,pp. The exact raw DSC values across $M{=}7/28/56/112$ (gate=pam, $r{=}32$) are 89.53/89.54/90.17/89.56\% on Kvasir-SEG and 89.41/89.55/89.05/89.51\% on ISIC 2018. Across all three datasets, optimal windowing outperforms the baseline DA-TransUNet metrics in Tables~\ref{tab:synapse}--\ref{tab:isic} by $+$1.14\% (Synapse, $M{=}28$), $+$1.73\% (Kvasir-SEG, $M{=}56$), and $+$0.67\% (ISIC 2018, $M{=}28$).

We formalize this cross-task behavior through the empirical metric \textbf{Global Context Sensitivity (GCS)}, defined as the performance span over the window sweep:
\begin{equation}
  \Delta\text{DSC} = \max_{M} \text{DSC}(M) - \min_{M} \text{DSC}(M),
\end{equation}
measured under uniform gate=pam, $r{=}32$. Empirically, $\Delta\text{DSC} = 2.30$\,pp on Synapse, $0.64$\,pp on Kvasir-SEG, and $0.50$\,pp on ISIC~2018---reflecting a 3.6$\times$ and 4.6$\times$ ratio relative to polyp and lesion segmentation, respectively. Rather than serving as an a priori predictive descriptor, GCS provides a quantified diagnostic of how strongly model accuracy is coupled to spatial context scale. High GCS reflects a sharp optimization landscape requiring careful window tuning, whereas low GCS demonstrates broad stability across receptive scopes. Importantly, high GCS does not indicate that approximation degrades performance, but rather characterizes performance sensitivity to window calibration.

These differences align with the contrasting anatomical context requirements of the tasks. Multi-organ abdominal CT segmentation involves complex inter-organ spatial dependencies, where both overly localized and overly broad receptive fields prove suboptimal. In contrast, polyp and skin lesion segmentation are heavily driven by local texture, color contrast, and boundary continuity, where windowed attention reliably preserves essential discriminatory cues.

Attention contribution maps corroborate this interpretation. Comparing windowed attention ($M{=}7$) with map-wide attention ($M{=}112$) on Kvasir-SEG polyp images, windowed attention concentrates 62.3\% of its total activation mass on the polyp target compared to 34.2\% for map-wide attention, exhibiting higher concentration in 9 of 10 evaluated samples (Figure~\ref{fig:attn_map}). Localized windowing naturally focuses attention along lesion boundaries while suppressing diffuse background responses.

\subsection{Routing Mechanism Ablation}
\label{sec:collapse}

We analyze branch routing by evaluating PAM-only, CAM-only, and learnable gating on Synapse under fixed $M{=}7, r{=}32, G{=}8$ (Table~\ref{tab:gate_ablation}).

\begin{table}[ht]
  \caption{Gate Mode Ablation on Synapse ($M$=7, $r$=32, $G$=8)}
  \label{tab:gate_ablation}
  \begin{center}
  \setlength{\tabcolsep}{4pt}
  \small
  \begin{tabular}{llcc}
    \toprule
    Gate Mode & $\mathbf{g}$ & DSC (\%) $\uparrow$ & HD95 (mm) $\downarrow$ \\
    \midrule
    gate=pam   & $\mathbf{1}$              & \textbf{78.64} & \underline{31.09} \\
    gate=cam   & $\mathbf{0}$              & \underline{78.26}  & \textbf{30.59} \\
    gate=learn & ${\approx}0.5\cdot\mathbf{1}^*$ & 77.78          & 34.29 \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \end{center}
  \vspace{-0.3em}
  \footnotesize{$^*$Learned per-channel gate values converged near 0.5 (Figure~\ref{fig:gate_analysis}).}
\end{table}

\begin{figure}[ht]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/fig_entropy_scatter.png}
  \caption{Mean gate value $g$ vs.\ feature entropy $H(F)$ across ApproxDABlock locations ($M{=}7$, gate=learn): bottleneck, 28$^2$, 56$^2$, and 112$^2$ skips. Across all locations, the mean gate exhibits minimal sample variance ($\Delta g \le 0.0002$) around 0.5. Spearman rank correlations with entropy are $r_s = 0.05, 0.23, 0.16, 0.79$, respectively.}
  \label{fig:gate_analysis}
\end{figure}

PAM-only routing attains the highest DSC (78.64\%), closely followed by CAM-only (78.26\%) and dynamic gating (77.78\%). In our Synapse experiments, the learnable gate converged to a near-uniform state ($g \approx 0.5$): Figure~\ref{fig:gate_analysis} shows that across all four block stages, mean gate values remain within $\Delta g \le 2{\times}10^{-4}$ of location-specific values near 0.5. Although gate values correlate with feature entropy at certain stages (up to $r_s{=}0.79$ at the $112^2$ skip), the absolute modulation range remains narrow across samples. A supplementary run with $M{=}14, r{=}64$ (78.04\% DSC) revealed consistent near-uniform routing. We document this as an empirical phenomenon in multi-organ CT training; exploring specialized regularization and alternative gating topologies presents an interesting direction for future work. Consequently, PAM-only routing serves as the standard configuration for cross-task window ablations.
