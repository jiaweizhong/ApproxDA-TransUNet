\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
This paper presented ApproxDA-TransUNet, a dual-attention approximation framework for medical image segmentation featuring independently controllable window size, projection rank, and channel grouping. Across three representative clinical benchmarks, ApproxDA-TransUNet improves Dice over the reported DA-TransUNet results with appropriately selected configurations, showing that the choice of attention approximation affects accuracy rather than acting solely as an efficiency mechanism.

Our systematic ablations further reveal that context sensitivity diverges substantially across clinical targets, a property we formalize via empirical Global Context Sensitivity (GCS): multi-organ CT segmentation is the most sensitive to the window size and benefits from map-wide context, whereas polyp and skin lesion targets are more robust across receptive window scales. Exact attention did not outperform the low-rank approximation. In addition, learnable dual-branch routing stays at near-uniform blending on multi-organ CT without surpassing single-branch routing.

\textbf{Limitations and Future Work.} Two limitations remain. First, the mechanism behind global context sensitivity is not yet understood: GCS summarizes how accuracy responds to the window size on the evaluated benchmarks, but why some targets benefit from map-wide context, and whether this can be predicted before an exhaustive sweep, remains open and needs validation across broader anatomical modalities and architectures. Second, the CNN--ViT backbone dominates the computational cost, so the savings of the approximated attention barely reach the whole network; lightweight backbones and specialized attention kernels are needed to turn them into end-to-end efficiency gains. We hope this work encourages principled, task-aware exploration of attention approximation across medical imaging domains.
