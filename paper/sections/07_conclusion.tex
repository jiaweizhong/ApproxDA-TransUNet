\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
This paper presented ApproxDA-TransUNet, a controlled approximation of dual attention that keeps the DA-TransUNet architecture and varies window size, projection rank, and channel grouping one at a time. Across Synapse, Kvasir-SEG, and ISIC 2018, the effect of the window size is strongly dataset-dependent: large on multi-organ CT and small on polyp and skin-lesion segmentation. With an appropriately selected window, ApproxDA-TransUNet improves Dice over DA-TransUNet re-trained under the same protocol, and its attention blocks require fewer FLOPs, although the end-to-end cost remains dominated by the backbone. These results suggest that attention approximation should be treated as a task-dependent design choice rather than an accuracy-neutral efficiency measure. Predicting the appropriate window without window ablations, and testing whether the observed sensitivity holds for other backbones and datasets, are left for future work.
