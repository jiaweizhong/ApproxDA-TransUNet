\begin{abstract}
Dual-attention mechanisms have demonstrated strong performance in medical image segmentation by jointly modeling long-range spatial and channel dependencies, but their quadratic complexity has motivated numerous approximation methods whose task-dependent behavior remains poorly understood. We propose ApproxDA-TransUNet, an efficient dual-attention approximation framework that replaces global attention with controllable windowed low-rank spatial attention and grouped channel attention. Unlike existing efficient attention methods that primarily pursue computational savings, ApproxDA-TransUNet also provides a controllable framework for systematically studying how attention approximation affects segmentation performance. Extensive experiments on Synapse, Kvasir-SEG, and ISIC 2018 demonstrate consistent improvements over the original DA-TransUNet, achieving 80.94\%, 90.17\%, and 89.58\% Dice, respectively. More importantly, our analysis reveals that the effectiveness of attention approximation depends on the contextual requirements of the target task. We term this phenomenon Global Context Sensitivity (GCS): tasks requiring broader contextual reasoning are substantially more sensitive to approximation scale than locally dominated tasks. We further show that the commonly adopted learnable dual-attention gate consistently collapses toward nearly uniform routing because of gradient symmetry, limiting the effectiveness of adaptive feature fusion. These findings suggest that attention approximation should be viewed not merely as an efficiency technique but as a task-dependent inductive bias. Our work provides both an effective segmentation framework and new insights into understanding attention approximation for medical image segmentation.
\end{abstract}
