\begin{abstract}
Dual-attention mechanisms have demonstrated strong performance in medical image segmentation by jointly modeling long-range spatial and channel dependencies, yet their quadratic complexity has motivated numerous approximation strategies whose task-dependent behavior remains poorly understood. To investigate this, we propose Approximate Dual Attention TransUNet (ApproxDA-TransUNet), a dual-attention approximation framework for hybrid CNN-Transformer architectures that integrates controllable windowed low-rank spatial attention with grouped channel attention. Beyond serving as an efficiency mechanism, ApproxDA-TransUNet provides a parametric platform to systematically evaluate how attention approximation interacts with representation capacity across diverse clinical segmentation tasks. On Synapse Multi-Organ CT, Kvasir-SEG polyp, and ISIC 2018 skin lesion benchmarks, ApproxDA-TransUNet achieves 80.94\%, 90.17\%, and 89.58\% Dice, improving Dice over DA-TransUNet across all three datasets. Furthermore, our empirical analysis reveals two key insights. First, window sensitivity varies markedly across tasks: the Dice spread across window sizes is up to 4.6$\times$ larger on multi-organ CT than on polyp and lesion targets, a characteristic we formalize as empirical Global Context Sensitivity (GCS). Second, in our multi-organ CT experiments, the learnable routing gate converged near uniform blending without outperforming single-branch spatial attention. These findings demonstrate that attention approximation functions not merely as a computational shortcut, but as a scale-dependent inductive bias whose optimal configuration is governed by task-specific context requirements.
\end{abstract}
