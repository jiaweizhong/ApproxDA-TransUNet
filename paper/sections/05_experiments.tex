\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We evaluate ApproxDA-TransUNet on three medical image segmentation benchmarks spanning different global context requirements. The experiments systematically examine computational efficiency, task-dependent approximation behavior across GCR regimes, the dynamics of symmetric gate routing, and the relationship between approximation strength and task context.

\subsection{Experimental Setup}

\textbf{Datasets.} We evaluate ApproxDA on three representative medical image segmentation benchmarks covering different contextual characteristics.

\textit{Synapse Multi-Organ CT}~\cite{landman2015miccai}: 30 abdominal CT scans with an 18/12 train/test split covering 8 organ classes (9 classes with background). Evaluation metrics: mean DSC and HD95.

\textit{Kvasir-SEG}~\cite{jha2020kvasir}: 1000 endoscopy polyp images (binary segmentation) with an 800/200 train/test split. Evaluation metrics: DSC and mIoU.

\textit{ISIC 2018}~\cite{codella2019isic}: 2594 dermoscopy skin lesion images (binary segmentation) with a 2075/519 train/test split (80/20). Evaluation metrics: DSC and mIoU.

Table~\ref{tab:datasets} summarizes the three datasets alongside their contextual characteristics and GCR classification, which motivates the cross-task analysis in Section~\ref{sec:analysis}.

\textbf{Implementation Details.} All experiments use an R50-ViT-B/16 backbone pretrained on ImageNet-21K. Training uses SGD (momentum 0.9, weight decay $10^{-4}$), initial learning rate 0.01 with polynomial decay, batch size 24, image size $224{\times}224$, 300 epochs, and seed 1234. The DA-TransUNet baseline runs on T4${\times}$1. ApproxDA-TransUNet runs on T4${\times}$2 via \texttt{torchrun} DDP with per-GPU batch 12 and NCCL all-reduce, under identical total batch size and base learning rate. Default ApproxDA configuration: $M{=}7$, $r{=}32$, $G{=}8$. Our DA-TransUNet re-run achieves validation DSC 0.795, consistent with the paper-reported 0.798, confirming faithful replication.

\subsection{Task-dependent Behavior of Attention Approximation}

We examine whether efficient attention approximation behaves consistently across medical segmentation tasks with different contextual requirements. Tables~\ref{tab:synapse}--\ref{tab:isic} compare ApproxDA-TransUNet against DA-TransUNet and established baselines on all three benchmarks.

\textbf{Synapse.} Table~\ref{tab:synapse} compares DSC and HD95 against 11 published methods. CNN-based methods achieve 76--78\% DSC. Transformer-based methods progressively improve global context modeling, with Swin-Unet (79.13\%) and DA-TransUNet (79.80\%) topping the leaderboard. ApproxDA-TransUNet (gate=pam, $M{=}7$, $r{=}32$) achieves 78.64\% DSC and 31.09\,mm HD95, surpassing all CNN-based baselines; the $-$1.16\% gap to DA-TransUNet indicates that high-GCR tasks require richer global spatial interaction than the approximated model can provide.

Strikingly, ApproxDA yields opposing effects across datasets: it consistently underperforms on Synapse ($-$1.16\% DSC) while achieving competitive or improved performance on Kvasir ($+$0.80\% DSC). This contrast is not attributable to hyperparameter differences---both experiments use the same $M$, $r$, and $G$---and instead reflects the intrinsically different degree to which each task relies on long-range spatial context.

\subsection{Analysis of Symmetric Gate Collapse}

Although the learnable gate (\texttt{gate=learn}) was designed to dynamically balance PAM and CAM branches, Table~\ref{tab:gate_ablation} reveals that it consistently ranks last on Synapse. The gate coefficient converges to $g{\approx}0.5$ and remains nearly constant throughout training, producing a fixed 50/50 blend that performs below both PAM-only (78.64\%) and CAM-only (78.26\%) configurations. This collapse is observed across both $M{=}7$ and $M{=}14$, confirming that it is a property of symmetric two-branch optimization rather than a consequence of limited receptive field. The gradient-level derivation is provided in Section~\ref{sec:collapse}.

\subsection{GCR and Approximation Effectiveness}

\textbf{Which approximation axis drives the performance gap?} Table~\ref{tab:window_ablation} isolates the contribution of spatial windowing by comparing windowed ($M{=}7$) against effectively global ($M{=}112$, window-clamped) attention under gate=pam. The $M{=}14$ result from the gate ablation (gate=learn) is also shown for reference to provide a denser sampling of window sizes.

\begin{table}[t]
  \caption{Spatial Approximation Ablation on Synapse ($G$=8)}
  \label{tab:window_ablation}
  \begin{center}
  \setlength{\tabcolsep}{2pt}
  \small
  \begin{tabular}{lccccc}
    \toprule
    Config & $M$ & $r$ & Gate & DSC (\%) $\uparrow$ & HD95 (mm) $\downarrow$ \\
    \midrule
    Windowed + low-rank & 7   & 32 & pam          & 78.64          & 31.09 \\
    Windowed + low-rank & 14  & 64 & learn$^\dag$ & 78.04          & \textbf{29.09} \\
    Global + low-rank   & 112 & 64 & pam          & \textbf{78.93} & 31.21 \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \end{center}
  \vspace{-0.3em}
  \footnotesize{$^\dag$Gate collapsed to $g{\approx}0.5$; gate mode differs from the other rows. $M{=}112$ via window clamping yields full-map attention at all scales. The $+$0.29\% DSC gain ($M{=}7{\to}112$, gate=pam) indicates low-rank projection is the binding constraint: even global attention ($r{=}64$) leaves a $-$0.87\% gap to DA-TransUNet. Note: $r$ co-varies with $M$; rows are not single-variable ablations.}
\end{table}

Removing windowing yields only $+$0.29\% DSC improvement, while the $-$0.87\% gap to DA-TransUNet persists under full-map attention. This reveals that low-rank projection---not spatial windowing---is the deeper bottleneck on high-GCR tasks: even global attention with $r{=}64$ cannot recover the attention fidelity lost by low-rank decomposition.

\textbf{Cross-task summary.} Table~\ref{tab:cross_task} consolidates results across all benchmarks. The direction of approximation's effect reverses with GCR: $-$1.16\% DSC on high-GCR Synapse versus $+$0.80\% DSC on low-GCR Kvasir-SEG. Multi-organ CT requires cross-organ anatomical reasoning that spatial approximation disrupts, while polyp segmentation depends primarily on local boundary contrast where windowed attention acts as an implicit regularizer. Figure~\ref{fig:qualitative} illustrates this context-dependent pattern qualitatively.

Together, the controlled experiments across datasets, gate modes, and window sizes establish a consistent picture: attention approximation behaves differently depending on the global context requirements of the target task. These observations motivate the formal GCR analysis in Section~\ref{sec:analysis}.
