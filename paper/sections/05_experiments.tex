\section{Results}
\label{sec:experiments}

We evaluate ApproxDA-TransUNet on three medical image segmentation benchmarks. Section~\ref{sec:understanding} provides mechanistic analysis of the observed task-dependent patterns.

\subsection{Experimental Setup}

\textbf{Datasets.} We evaluate ApproxDA-TransUNet on three representative medical image segmentation benchmarks covering different contextual characteristics.

\textit{Synapse Multi-Organ CT}~\cite{landman2015miccai}: 30 abdominal CT scans with an 18/12 train/test split covering 8 organ classes (9 classes with background). Evaluation metrics: mean DSC and HD95.

\textit{Kvasir-SEG}~\cite{jha2020kvasir}: 1000 endoscopy polyp images (binary segmentation) with an 800/200 train/test split. Evaluation metrics: DSC and mIoU.

\textit{ISIC 2018}~\cite{codella2019isic}: 2594 dermoscopy skin lesion images (binary segmentation) with a 2075/519 train/test split (80/20). Evaluation metrics: DSC and mIoU.

Table~\ref{tab:datasets} summarizes the three datasets alongside their contextual characteristics and CS classification, which motivates the cross-task analysis in Section~\ref{sec:understanding}.

\textbf{Implementation Details.} All experiments use an R50-ViT-B/16 backbone pretrained on ImageNet-21K. Training uses SGD with initial learning rate 0.01 and polynomial decay, batch size 24, image size $224{\times}224$, 300 epochs, and seed 1234. Default ApproxDA-TransUNet configuration: $M{=}7$, $r{=}32$, $G{=}8$.

\subsection{Comparison with State-of-the-Art}

Tables~\ref{tab:synapse}--\ref{tab:isic} compare ApproxDA-TransUNet against DA-TransUNet and established baselines on all three benchmarks.

\textbf{Synapse.} Table~\ref{tab:synapse} compares DSC and HD95 against 11 published methods. CNN-based methods achieve 76--78\% DSC. Transformer-based methods progressively improve global context modeling, with Swin-Unet (79.13\%) and DA-TransUNet (79.80\%) previously topping the leaderboard. ApproxDA-TransUNet (gate=pam, $M{=}28$, $r{=}32$) achieves \textbf{80.94\%} DSC and 27.49\,mm HD95---surpassing all baselines including DA-TransUNet by $+$1.14\%. The optimal window $M{=}28$ provides the best balance between local detail and global context; Section~\ref{sec:understanding} analyzes this task-dependent behavior in detail.

\textbf{Kvasir-SEG.} Table~\ref{tab:kvasir} compares polyp segmentation results against all six methods reported in~\cite{sun2024datransunet}. We re-ran DA-TransUNet under the same SGD/300ep protocol as ApproxDA-TransUNet to enable a direct comparison, obtaining 88.44\% DSC, 81.70\% mIoU, and 53.04\,mm HD95. ApproxDA-TransUNet (gate=pam, $M$=56) achieves 90.17\% DSC, 84.27\% mIoU, and 44.35\,mm HD95---outperforming the reproduced DA-TransUNet baseline (+1.73\% DSC, +2.57\% mIoU, $-$8.69\,mm HD95).

\textbf{ISIC 2018.} Table~\ref{tab:isic} reports skin lesion segmentation results against the same six baselines from~\cite{sun2024datransunet}. We re-ran DA-TransUNet under our protocol (SGD, 300ep, 80/20 split), obtaining 88.43\% DSC and 80.68\% mIoU. ApproxDA-TransUNet (gate=learn, $M{=}7$, $r{=}32$) achieves \textbf{89.58\%} DSC and \textbf{82.66\%} mIoU---outperforming the reproduced DA-TransUNet baseline by $+$1.15\% DSC and $+$1.98\% mIoU, and surpassing all published baselines including the paper-reported DA-TransUNet (88.88\%). This extends the low-CS pattern observed on Kvasir-SEG to a third benchmark.

With appropriate window selection, ApproxDA-TransUNet outperforms DA-TransUNet on all three datasets: $+$1.14\% DSC on Synapse ($M{=}28$), $+$1.73\% DSC on Kvasir ($M{=}56$), and $+$1.15\% DSC on ISIC 2018 ($M{=}7$, gate=learn). The sensitivity to window choice differs markedly across the two datasets with full ablations: Synapse DSC spans 2.30\% across $M$ values while Kvasir spans only 0.64\%---a 3.6$\times$ ratio reflecting the different context sensitivity of the two tasks.


\textbf{Cross-task summary.} Table~\ref{tab:cross_task} consolidates results. With task-appropriate window selection, ApproxDA-TransUNet improves over DA-TransUNet on all three benchmarks: $+$1.14\% on empirically high-CS Synapse, $+$1.73\% on empirically low-CS Kvasir, and $+$1.15\% on empirically low-CS ISIC. The sensitivity ratio (2.30\% vs.\ 0.64\% on Synapse vs.\ Kvasir) is the primary empirical operationalization of CS. Figure~\ref{fig:qualitative} shows qualitative examples.

\subsection{Efficiency Analysis}

Table~\ref{tab:efficiency} compares the computational characteristics of ApproxDA-TransUNet and DA-TransUNet on Synapse. Although per-GPU VRAM is reduced substantially---from 11.5\,GB to 6.4\,GB---total GFLOPs are slightly higher (32.1 vs.\ 30.2) because the shared ViT backbone dominates total computation and projection overhead partially offsets decoder-level savings. The reduced memory footprint enables multi-GPU distributed training via DDP, which DA-TransUNet does not support.
