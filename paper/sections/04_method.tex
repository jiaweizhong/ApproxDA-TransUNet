\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

This section introduces the overall architecture of ApproxDA-TransUNet and the placement of ApproxDABlocks within it, details the three independently controllable approximation axes implemented in each block, and analyzes their computational characteristics.

\subsection{Architecture Overview}

Figure~\ref{fig:framework} illustrates the overall architecture: ApproxDA-TransUNet adopts the hybrid CNN--ViT encoder-decoder structure of DA-TransUNet~\cite{sun2024datransunet}, with an R50-ViT-B/16 backbone pretrained on ImageNet-21K. Three CNN encoder stages produce features at $112{\times}112$, $56{\times}56$, and $28{\times}28$ resolutions (strides 2, 4, and 8 for a $224{\times}224$ input), while a 12-layer ViT processes $14{\times}14$ patch tokens at the bottleneck. While DA-TransUNet integrates attention modules at three stages (the bottleneck and the 512- and 256-channel skip paths), ApproxDA-TransUNet uniformly equips all three skip connections (512 channels at $28{\times}28$, 256 at $56{\times}56$, and 64 at $112{\times}112$) as well as the bottleneck (768 channels at $14{\times}14$) with ApproxDABlocks, providing comprehensive multi-scale context modulation. In contrast to channel-compression designs that project intermediate features to $C/16$, each ApproxDABlock operates on the full $C$ channel capacity during token projection to preserve representational fidelity. Each block routes activations through a Low-Rank Windowed PAM branch and a Grouped CAM branch, fused via a configurable gating vector $\mathbf{g}$. Figure~\ref{fig:approxda_block} details the internal structure of the ApproxDABlock and its three parameterized approximation axes.

\subsection{Attention Approximation Formulation}

Standard dual attention performs dense spatial and channel affinity modeling over the entire feature map, incurring quadratic complexity in both spatial resolution and channel count. ApproxDA-TransUNet structures this complexity along three orthogonal axes, each governing a distinct dimension of the approximation:

\textbf{Spatial scope} (window size $M$) controls the receptive range of spatial attention. Full global attention ($M{=}H$) models all pairwise spatial dependencies, whereas local $M{\times}M$ window partitioning restricts attention to local neighborhoods at reduced complexity.

\textbf{Representation fidelity} (rank $r$) controls the subspace precision within each window. Low-rank projection ($r \ll M^2$) compresses the key--value affinity matrix into an informative subspace, while higher $r$ approaches exact intra-window attention.

\textbf{Channel interaction} (groups $G$) controls the cross-channel dependency span in CAM. Partitioning $C$ channels into $G$ independent groups constrains channel correlations to within-group interactions, scaling the interaction granularity.

Because these parameters are orthogonally parameterized, any element in $\{M,\,r,\,G\}$ can be independently ablated while maintaining the others constant.

\subsection{Spatial Approximation}

Instead of computing global position attention, ApproxDA-TransUNet partitions the feature map into non-overlapping windows of size $M{\times}M$. Attention is computed independently inside each local window, reducing complexity from $\mathcal{O}(N^2C)$ to $\mathcal{O}(NM^2C)$, where $N = HW$.

\textbf{Window partitioning.} Following~\cite{liu2021swin}, we partition the $C{\times}H{\times}W$ feature map into $n_W$ non-overlapping $M{\times}M$ windows, with $M \leftarrow \min(M, H, W)$. For one window, let $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times N_w}$ with $N_w = M^2$ denote its features, and let
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{Q} = \mathbf{W}_Q\mathbf{X}, \quad \mathbf{K} = \mathbf{W}_K\mathbf{X}, \quad \mathbf{V} = \mathbf{W}_V\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times N_w}
  \label{eq:qkv}
\end{equation}
be the query, key, and value features produced by $1{\times}1$ convolutions $\mathbf{W}_Q, \mathbf{W}_K, \mathbf{W}_V \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times C}$ (with bias).

\textbf{Low-rank projection.} Following~\cite{wang2020linformer}, keys and values inside each window are compressed along the token axis by a learned projection $\mathbf{P} \in \mathbb{R}^{N_w \times r}$, shared by keys and values and across windows within a block:
\begin{equation}
  \tilde{\mathbf{K}} = \mathbf{K}\mathbf{P}, \quad \tilde{\mathbf{V}} = \mathbf{V}\mathbf{P} \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times r}.
  \label{eq:lowrank}
\end{equation}
Each of the $N_w$ queries attends to the $r$ projected keys:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \mathbf{A} &= \operatorname{softmax}\!\big(\mathbf{Q}^{\top}\tilde{\mathbf{K}}\big) \in \mathbb{R}^{N_w \times r}, \\
  \mathbf{E} &= \alpha\,\big(\mathbf{A}\tilde{\mathbf{V}}^{\top}\big)^{\!\top} + \mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times N_w},
\end{aligned}
  \label{eq:pam_lr}
\end{equation}
where the softmax normalizes each row across the $r$ projected keys, without temperature scaling (consistent with standard PAM~\cite{fu2019dual}), and $\alpha$ is a learned scalar initialized to 0. Each window output $\mathbf{E}$ is reshaped to $C{\times}M{\times}M$, and all windows are reassembled into the $H{\times}W$ grid. The full $N_w{\times}N_w$ affinity is thus substituted by an $N_w{\times}r$ matrix, lowering attention complexity to $\mathcal{O}(NrC)$. When $M$ is clamped to match feature dimensions, the leading $N_w$ rows of $\mathbf{P}$ are utilized; setting $M{=}112$ establishes global spatial scope per feature map while preserving low-rank ($r{=}32$) projection. Together, windowing and low-rank projection reduce the theoretical attention-matrix complexity from $\mathcal{O}(N^2)$ to $\mathcal{O}(Nr)$ (${\sim}390{\times}$ reduction in affinity matrix entries at $112{\times}112$ with $r{=}32$; see Section~\ref{sec:efficiency}).

\subsection{Channel Approximation}

The standard Channel Attention Module computes a full $C{\times}C$ channel correlation matrix at $\mathcal{O}(C^2N)$ complexity. ApproxDA-TransUNet approximates this by partitioning $C$ channels into $G$ groups of width $C_g = C/G$ and computing independent per-group attention. For group $g$ with channel slices $\mathbf{A}^{(g)}_1, \dots, \mathbf{A}^{(g)}_{C_g} \in \mathbb{R}^{N}$ of the block input,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  x^{(g)}_{ij} &= \frac{\exp(\mathbf{A}^{(g)}_i \cdot \mathbf{A}^{(g)}_j)}{\sum_{k=1}^{C_g}\exp(\mathbf{A}^{(g)}_i \cdot \mathbf{A}^{(g)}_k)}, \\
  \mathbf{E}^{(g)}_j &= \beta \sum_{i=1}^{C_g} x^{(g)}_{ij}\,\mathbf{A}^{(g)}_i + \mathbf{A}^{(g)}_j,
\end{aligned}
  \label{eq:groupedcam}
\end{equation}
with a learned scalar $\beta$ initialized to 0 and total cost $\mathcal{O}(C^2N/G)$. In Eq.~\eqref{eq:groupedcam}, softmax normalizes along keys to yield row-stochastic attention weights before aggregation. In our subsequent primary benchmarks, PAM-only routing is utilized, focusing analysis on spatial context modulation.

\subsection{Routing Strategy}

The routing mechanism provides a configurable \texttt{gate\_mode} to blend spatial and channel representations. PAM and CAM branch outputs $\mathbf{P}$ and $\mathbf{C}$ (both $C{\times}H{\times}W$) are fused via:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{y} = \mathbf{W}_f\!\left(\mathbf{g} \odot \mathbf{P} + (\mathbf{1}-\mathbf{g}) \odot \mathbf{C}\right) + \mathbf{x},
  \label{eq:fusion}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{W}_f$ is a $1{\times}1$ convolution and $\mathbf{g} \in [0,1]^C$ is a per-channel routing vector broadcast spatially, determined by the \texttt{gate\_mode} setting:

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
\texttt{pam}:   & $\mathbf{g} = \mathbf{1}$ \quad (PAM only) \\
\texttt{cam}:   & $\mathbf{g} = \mathbf{0}$ \quad (CAM only) \\
\texttt{fixed}: & $\mathbf{g} = 0.5\cdot\mathbf{1}$ \quad (equal blend) \\
\texttt{learn}: & $\mathbf{g} = \sigma(\mathbf{W}_g\,\mathrm{GAP}(\mathbf{x})) \in (0,1)^C$ \\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

In \texttt{learn} mode, $\mathbf{g}$ is derived per sample from globally pooled features ($\mathrm{GAP}$) via linear projection $\mathbf{W}_g$ and sigmoid activation $\sigma$. This formulation establishes a unified parametric interface to systematically ablate branch contributions without altering backbone topology.

The network is trained with a composite Dice and cross-entropy objective with equal weighting:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L} = \tfrac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}_{\text{Dice}} + \tfrac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}},
  \label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
maintaining standard practice for medical segmentation benchmarks~\cite{chen2021transunet,sun2024datransunet}.
