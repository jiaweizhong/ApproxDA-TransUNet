\section{GCS Mechanism: Causal Elimination and Semantic Spatial Dependency}
\label{sec:gcs_mechanism}

Section~\ref{sec:obs2} characterized GCS empirically through window-size ablations. This section investigates the underlying mechanism through a systematic causal elimination study: five candidate explanatory factors are computed from ground-truth masks alone (no training required), assessed against the known GCS ordering, and ruled out one by one, converging on Semantic Spatial Dependency (SSD) as the unifying explanation.

\subsection{Candidate Metrics and Evaluation Protocol}

We compute the following metrics from 200 randomly sampled training masks per dataset:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{SC1 --- Foreground Ratio:} Fraction of pixels belonging to any foreground class. Tests whether GCS is driven by target scale.
  \item \textbf{SC2 --- Boundary Complexity:} Mean isoperimetric ratio ($P^2/4\pi A$) per connected component. Tests whether GCS is driven by boundary irregularity.
  \item \textbf{SC3 --- Spatial Entropy:} Normalized entropy of the foreground pixel distribution over an $8{\times}8$ spatial grid. Tests whether GCS is driven by foreground dispersion.
  \item \textbf{SC4 --- Window Crossing Ratio (WCR):} Fraction of foreground pixels belonging to components spanning more than one $M{\times}M$ window ($M{=}28$ pixels, the pixel-space equivalent of the model's $M{=}7$ feature-space window at 4${\times}$ Swin downsampling). Tests whether individual target components crossing window boundaries drives GCS.
  \item \textbf{SC5 --- Inter-class Window Separation:} Fraction of class-centroid pairs falling in different $M{\times}M$ windows (multi-class datasets; zero by construction for binary tasks). Measures whether distinct semantic structures require cross-window attention.
\end{itemize}

For SC1--SC4 (rule-out checks), a successful elimination requires Spearman $\rho \approx 0$ or wrong-direction correlation against the known GCS ordering (Synapse 2.30~pp $>$ ACDC 0.73~pp $\approx$ ISIC 0.70~pp $\approx$ Kvasir 0.64~pp $\approx$ CVC 0.62~pp, five datasets). An additional proxy search identifies $n_\text{classes}$---the mean number of foreground semantic classes per image---as a training-free GCS predictor. Table~\ref{tab:causal} summarizes all results.

\subsection{Ruling Out Alternative Explanations}

\textbf{SC1 and SC3 are ruled out.} Foreground ratio ($\rho{=}-0.60$) and spatial entropy ($\rho{=}-0.60$) both have wrong-direction correlations across all four datasets. Synapse has the \emph{smallest} foreground coverage (7.7\%) and the \emph{lowest} spatial entropy, yet the highest GCS. GCS is not driven by how large or how uniformly distributed the foreground is.

\textbf{SC4 saturation rules out component-level window crossing.} At $M{=}28$ pixels, WCR saturates near 1.0 for all four datasets. Even a single large polyp crosses window boundaries in pixel space, independent of task type. The saturation itself is informative: \emph{whether an individual target region crosses window boundaries does not predict GCS}. The discriminative mechanism must operate at a higher semantic level than individual components.

\textbf{SC2 does not survive the four-dataset test.} On three datasets, boundary complexity ranked Synapse~$>$~ISIC~$>$~Kvasir ($\rho{=}+1.00$). Adding ACDC (cardiac MRI, SC2${=}0.928$, low GCS) drops this to $\rho{=}+0.40$, ruling it out. The three-dataset result was an imaging-modality confound: CT physics produces high boundary contrast regardless of class count, which happened to correlate with GCS when all three datasets were compared---ACDC breaks this spurious alignment.

\subsection{Semantic Spatial Dependency as the Unifying Mechanism}
\label{sec:ssd}

The surviving evidence points to a single mechanism. SC5---inter-class window separation---achieves $\rho{=}+0.95$ across four datasets, the strongest predictor among all five metrics. Synapse's SC5 is 0.9996: windowed attention at $M{=}28$ cannot simultaneously attend to nearly all organ pairs. ACDC's SC5 is 0.547: cardiac structures are partially separated at this scale. Binary datasets have SC5${=}0$ by construction. The proxy metric $n_\text{classes}$ ($\rho{=}+0.87$, three datasets) captures the same structure but is not the cause---ACDC ($n_\text{classes}{\approx}3$, high as Synapse yet low GCS) directly falsifies it.

We term the underlying mechanism \emph{Semantic Spatial Dependency (SSD)}: the extent to which accurate segmentation of each class requires locating it relative to other semantic structures. Multi-organ CT exemplifies high SSD---the pancreas must be located relative to the stomach, duodenum, and adjacent vessels; no single window captures all cross-organ context simultaneously. Binary segmentation tasks have zero inter-class SSD by definition. The causal chain is:

\begin{align*}
  \underbrace{\text{SSD}}_{\text{task property}}
  &\;\to\;
  \underbrace{\text{cross-structure reasoning}}_{\text{attention requirement}} \\
  &\;\to\;
  \underbrace{\text{sensitivity to window scale}}_{\text{= GCS}}.
\end{align*}

The empirical proxy $n_\text{classes}$ correlates with SSD because more classes generally implies richer inter-class spatial dependencies, but class count is not the cause---and this distinction is directly validated by an additional experiment.

\textbf{ACDC as a discriminating test case.} To separate $n_\text{classes}$ from SSD as the true driver, we ran the same window ablation ($M{\in}\{7,28,56,112\}$, gate=pam) on the ACDC cardiac MRI benchmark~\cite{bernard2018deep}, a 4-class task (background, RV, Myo, LV). If class count drives GCS, ACDC should show intermediate-to-high sensitivity. Instead, $\Delta\text{DSC}{=}0.73$\,pp---indistinguishable from the binary datasets Kvasir-SEG ($0.64$\,pp), CVC-ClinicDB ($0.62$\,pp), and ISIC~2018 ($0.70$\,pp), and far below Synapse ($2.30$\,pp).

SC5 quantifies this directly. ACDC's inter-class window separation is 0.547 at $M{=}28$---cardiac structures (RV, Myo, LV) occupy adjacent but overlapping windows due to their concentric anatomy---compared to Synapse's near-complete separation (SC5${=}0.9996$) and binary tasks' structural zero. Crucially, GCS exhibits a strongly non-linear response: the drop from SC5${=}0.9996$ to $0.547$ (Synapse $\to$ ACDC) produces a 1.57\,pp fall in GCS (2.30 $\to$ 0.73\,pp), while the further drop from $0.547$ to $0$ (ACDC $\to$ binary tasks: Kvasir 0.64\,pp, CVC 0.62\,pp, ISIC 0.70\,pp) yields negligible additional change (${\approx}0.09$--$0.11$\,pp). Near-complete inter-class separation---not merely multi-class structure---is what triggers high GCS.

The ACDC result therefore refines the SSD theory: the key quantity is not class count per se but \emph{degree of inter-class spatial separation relative to the attention window}. A multi-class task with compact, stereotyped anatomy (SC5${<}0.6$) behaves identically to a binary task in terms of GCS. SC5, computable from annotation masks alone, provides a training-free SSD estimate that predicts GCS tier without running a single experiment.

\textbf{Practical implication.} GCS-aware window selection reduces to an annotation-level check: tasks with near-complete inter-class window separation (SC5${>}0.8$, e.g.\ multi-organ CT) are predicted high-GCS and require window tuning. Tasks with low or intermediate SC5---whether binary or multi-class with compact anatomy---are predicted low-GCS and largely window-robust.

\textbf{Per-organ validation within Synapse.} The global GCS ($\Delta\text{DSC}{=}2.30$\,pp) aggregates over eight organs whose individual window sensitivities span 0.39--6.45\,pp (Table~\ref{tab:per_organ}), providing within-task evidence for SSD at the organ level. Gallbladder shows the highest sensitivity (6.45\,pp): it is small, partially occluded under the liver, and its localization requires the global liver--gallbladder spatial relationship---a textbook high-SSD structure. Stomach (4.97\,pp) has highly variable shape and fill state, requiring surrounding organ context for boundary delineation. By contrast, the liver (0.39\,pp) is the dominant abdominal landmark---identifiable at any window scale without cross-organ cues---and the aorta (1.89\,pp) is a cylindrical structure running along a fixed anatomical axis, similarly window-robust.

The pancreas is a special case: it achieves only 60--63\% DSC across \emph{all} four window sizes, so its $\Delta\text{DSC}$ (2.05\,pp) reflects a consistent approximation ceiling rather than low SSD. Where the model achieves reasonable baseline performance, $\Delta\text{DSC}$ reliably tracks anatomical SSD.
